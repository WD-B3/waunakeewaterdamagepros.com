A failed appliance line is an emergency because water keeps flowing until someone shuts it off. Turn off the valve behind the washer, dishwasher or refrigerator, unplug the appliance only if the floor is dry, and call the 24/7 line. Pull the appliance out only when you can do so without standing in water.

Appliance leak water damage is the wetting of floors and walls after a supply hose, drain hose or internal part fails. Washer hoses, dishwasher supply lines and refrigerator ice maker lines are the most common culprits, and drain-side failures can release gray water.

The water often reaches the cabinet bases and under flooring before it is noticed. For leaks at the sink, see kitchen water damage, and for a failed tank, see water heater leaks.

Appliance leak: what to do first

Stop the flow and keep the floor safe.

  • Close the water valve at the wall. For a washer, close both hot and cold valves.
  • Unplug the appliance only if you can reach the outlet while standing on a dry floor, otherwise cut the breaker.
  • Move the appliance away from the wall if you can do it safely, to find the source.
  • Photograph the hose, valve and wet area before cleanup.

Why small appliance leaks cause big floor damage

A washer hose that bursts under pressure can release water for the length of time before anyone notices, and a dishwasher that leaks for a week can warp the floor under it without leaving a puddle. The water follows the path of least resistance, often under flooring and into the subfloor, where it dries slowly.

Laminate and engineered flooring swell at their seams, hardwood cups, and sheet vinyl traps water beneath it. That is why a floor that looks only damp on top may need more attention below, and the hardwood floor page covers what happens to wood.

How we handle an appliance leak

  • The crew extracts standing water and checks under the appliance, the cabinet base and the surrounding flooring.
  • Moisture meters confirm which materials are wet, and thermal imaging shows moisture behind baseboards.
  • Wet baseboards and soaked material that cannot dry are removed, and the remaining area is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Readings are logged until the area is dry, and the record supports your insurance claim.

If the water reached a room below, the ceiling repair page explains that part.

Sudden appliance failures are often covered, while a leak that ran for weeks may not be. Ask your insurer and keep the failed hose or part.

Checks that prevent appliance leaks

Replace old rubber supply hoses with braided stainless lines, and look at the connections every few months. Do not run the washer or dishwasher when no one is home. A shutoff valve that is easy to reach, and a drip tray or sensor under the machine, turn a flood into a puddle.

Appliance leak questions

Do you handle appliance leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Waunakee?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day and same-day response is offered when crews are available. Close the valve and call.

Should I keep the failed hose or part?

Yes. Keep it and photograph it, since your insurer may ask to see what failed.

Can you check under my dishwasher or washer for hidden water?

Yes. Moisture meters and thermal imaging can find water behind and under appliances without tearing out finished surfaces first.

Does insurance cover a washing machine hose burst?

Sudden hose failures are often covered, while slow leaks and wear usually are not. Check your policy.
