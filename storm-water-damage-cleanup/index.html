Storm water damage is an emergency because the opening that let the rain in may still be open. If a roof, window or door has been breached, call the 24/7 line and ask about temporary protection such as tarping, then stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings. Keep clear of downed lines, and call the electric utility or 911 for any live wire.

Storm water damage cleanup covers the water that enters through a damaged roof, siding, window or door during a storm. It is different from flooding, where water rises from the ground, and it is usually cleaner at the start, though insulation and attic debris can change that.

If the storm also raised creek or surface water, see flood damage cleanup. Water that reached the ceiling is covered in ceiling repair.

After a storm: first steps

Make the building safe before you clean.

  • Stay clear of downed power lines and call the utility or 911.
  • Keep out of rooms with a bulging ceiling or standing water near electrical.
  • Cover openings with a tarp if you can do it safely from the ground, otherwise call for temporary protection.
  • Photograph the roof, siding, windows and every wet room before cleanup.

Dane County's August 2018 flooding followed record rainfall in the area, and county reports counted more than $154 million in damage and 1,544 residences affected. It showed how a single storm can overwhelm drainage across many neighborhoods at once, including places far from any mapped floodplain.

Madison averages about 37 inches of precipitation a year, and the nearest official benchmark for Waunakee is Madison. A roof or window that sheds a normal storm can fail under wind-driven rain, so check flashings and seals after severe weather.

How we handle storm water damage

  • Dispatch can arrange temporary protection to limit more rain entering while repairs are planned.
  • Crews extract standing water, and moisture mapping finds wet insulation and drywall.
  • Wet insulation and drywall that cannot dry are removed, and the structure is dried to a measured standard.
  • The roofing and exterior repair belongs to a roofer or contractor, and we coordinate around it.

Roof and exterior repair

Wind and hail damage is generally covered by homeowners policies, while flood is not. Ask your insurer which applies, and document before cleanup.

Temporary protection and your roofer

After a storm, roofers are in high demand and schedules fill up. A temporary tarp or board-up limits additional rain while you wait for a permanent repair. Keep any receipts for temporary protection, because insurers generally expect reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

Get a written estimate for the permanent repair, and keep the old materials if the adjuster wants to see them. Our crews work with your roofer so drying does not stall while the roof is repaired.

Storm documentation checklist

  • Photographs of the roof, siding, windows and gutters from the ground.
  • Photographs of every room with water, including ceilings and walls.
  • Notes on the date, time and type of storm.
  • A list of damaged belongings, with age and value.
  • The adjuster's name and claim number, once assigned.

Storm water damage questions

Do you handle storm damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Waunakee?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24 hours a day and same-day response is offered when crews are available. Storms often hit after hours, so the line is staffed.

Can you tarp a damaged roof?

Dispatch can arrange temporary protection. The permanent roof repair is a roofer's job.

Is storm water damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Wind and hail damage is generally covered, and flooding generally is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Is storm damage the same as flood damage?

No. Storm damage is rain entering through a breach. Flood damage is rising or flowing outside water, and it is classified differently.
