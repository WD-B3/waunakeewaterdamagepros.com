How to Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Waunakee Home

A pre-trip checklist for winter travel, long vacations and second homes.

By the Waunakee Water Damage Pros teamUpdated November 2026

A leak at home is bad. A leak at home while no one is there is worse, because it can run for days before it is noticed. Travelers, snowbirds and second-home owners are the people most likely to come back to a flooded house, and a few steps before leaving cut that risk.

This guide is a checklist to run through before a trip. It is general advice for Waunakee and the nearby towns, and a plumber can help with the bigger items such as shutoff valves and leak sensors.

If you come home to water, shut off the main, photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line.

The pre-trip checklist

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days.
  2. Leave the heat on, set no lower than the level your plumber recommends in winter.
  3. Test the sump pump and make sure the discharge is clear.
  4. Check washing machine hoses and dishwasher lines for bulges or cracks.
  5. Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and close exterior faucets.
  6. Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house and give them the dispatch number.

Winter trips need extra care

Madison's January average is 19.4 degrees, and a furnace failure while you are away is the classic way to come home to a frozen pipe flood. A smart thermostat or a temperature sensor with alerts tells you if the house is getting cold. A neighbor checking in is a reliable backup.

See the pages on frozen pipes and burst pipes for what to do if it happens.

Insurance and vacancy

Some policies limit coverage for damage when the home has been vacant or unoccupied for a set period, and some require reasonable steps such as keeping heat on. Read the vacancy and unoccupancy language in your policy, and ask your agent if you will be away for weeks.

The Wisconsin insurance commissioner advises homeowners to check what their policy covers before a loss. For second homes, ask whether the policy treats it as a seasonal dwelling.

Away from Waunakee, Dane or DeForest

Spring and summer trips bring storm and sump risks, because Madison averages about 37 inches of precipitation a year and a heavy rain can overwhelm a pump while you are gone. Dane County's August 2018 flooding hit during the summer. A battery backup and a neighbor with the dispatch number are worth the effort.

Owners of seasonal homes in Mazomanie and Dane have the same exposure, and many have private wells and septic systems that need their own checks. Ask a plumber what to do before closing a seasonal home.

What to do if you come home to water

Do not walk into standing water near electrical. Shut off the main if you can reach it safely, photograph the damage and call dispatch. Tell the call-taker how long the house has been empty, since that affects the scope. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Gadgets that help while you are away

  • Leak sensors that send alerts to your phone.
  • Smart thermostats with low-temperature alerts.
  • Automatic shutoff valves that close when they detect flow.
  • Battery backups for sump pumps.
  • Power outage alerts so you know if the furnace or pump has lost power.

Make it easy for the person checking in

Give your neighbor a short list: where the main valve is, where the thermostat is, what to do if they see water and the dispatch number. Tell them to call you and the dispatch line, not to wait. If they find water, their first act should be to shut off the main and take photos.

A key, a short note and a thank-you gift go a long way. This small favor often prevents a very large loss.

After you return

Walk through the whole house, including the basement and the attic hatch, before you unpack. Check the sump pump, the water heater and under sinks. Turn the main back on slowly and watch for drips. If anything looks wrong, shut it off again and call.

A note on appliances

Washers and dishwashers should not run while you sleep or travel. Many leaks happen mid-cycle, when the hose is under pressure. Closing the supply valves after the last load takes ten seconds and removes the most common cause of a flood.

Away-from-home water damage questions

Should I shut off the main water when I travel?

For trips of more than a few days, yes. It is the single most effective step against a burst pipe running unnoticed.

What if I leave the heat low in winter?

Ask your plumber for a safe minimum. Pipes in exterior walls can freeze if the house gets too cold.

Does insurance still cover a vacant home in Wisconsin?

It depends on the policy. Some limit coverage after a period of vacancy, so ask your agent.

Who should I give the dispatch number to?

A neighbor or friend who will check the home, and anyone who has a key.
