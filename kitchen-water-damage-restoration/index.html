Kitchens combine plumbing, appliances and finished surfaces, so a small leak does expensive damage. Cabinet bases wick water, flooring swells and the subfloor holds moisture under everything. If you notice a soft cabinet floor, lifted flooring or a damp smell, shut off the water to the fixture and call dispatch.

Kitchen water damage restoration is the drying and repair of cabinets, flooring, walls and subfloor after a kitchen leak. Typical sources include sink supply lines, drain connections, dishwashers and refrigerator ice maker lines.

Appliance failures are covered in more depth on the appliance leak page, and the floor side is covered in hardwood floor repair.

What can be saved in a wet kitchen

Solid wood and plywood cabinet boxes can often be dried if the water is caught quickly. Particleboard and MDF cabinets swell and lose strength, and they usually cannot be saved. Countertops are rarely damaged by water, but they may have to be removed if the cabinets under them are replaced.

Flooring depends on the type. Tile survives better than laminate or vinyl plank, which can trap water below. Hardwood may be dried with specialized equipment if it is caught early, which is covered on the hardwood page.

How the restoration proceeds

  1. Water is extracted and the source is repaired by a plumber or appliance technician.
  2. Toe-kicks and baseboards come off so air can reach the cabinet bases and wall cavities.
  3. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cabinets, walls and subfloor with logged readings.
  4. Cabinets and flooring that cannot dry are removed, and repairs follow once readings meet the standard.

Sudden kitchen leaks are often covered by homeowners policies, and slow leaks often are not. Ask your insurer, and keep the failed part.

Countertops, appliances and what to do first

Stone and solid-surface counters rarely suffer from a leak, but the cabinets under them might. Moving or lifting counters is costly, so the crew looks for the least invasive way to dry the cabinet bases, often by removing the toe-kick and drilling small vent holes to push air into the cavity.

Appliances can stay in place if the floor under them is dry. If the leak came from the appliance itself, a technician should check it before it is used again.

Kitchen leak prevention checks

  • Look under the sink each month for dampness, drips or swollen cabinet floors.
  • Replace rubber washer and dishwasher hoses with braided lines every few years.
  • Check the refrigerator ice maker line for kinks or leaks.
  • Know where the shutoff valves for the sink and dishwasher are.
  • Keep a small tray or sensor under the sink to catch drips.

Why the first day matters in a kitchen

A kitchen has the most finished, expensive material per square foot in the house. Cabinets, counters, flooring and appliances all sit within a few feet of each other, so a leak reaches several of them at once. Fast extraction and airflow into the cabinet bases are what separate a dried kitchen from a gutted one.

Photograph the cabinet interiors before anything is removed, and keep the toe-kick and any swollen panels. They show your insurer what the water did and where it traveled.
