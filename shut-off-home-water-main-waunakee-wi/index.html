How to Shut Off Your Home's Water Main in Waunakee

The most useful minute you can spend before a leak: find the valve and learn to close it.

By the Waunakee Water Damage Pros teamUpdated November 2026

When a pipe fails, the single most important action is shutting off the water. Every minute the water runs adds to the damage, and a homeowner who knows where the main valve is can limit a major loss to a small one. Most people never look for it until the emergency.

This guide explains where the main shutoff is usually found, how the common valve types work and what to do once the water is off. It is general information, and a plumber can tag the valve and check it works.

Do this once, today, and you will thank yourself later. If water is already spreading, shut it off and then call the 24/7 line.

Where to look

  1. In the basement or utility room, usually on the wall facing the street where the water line enters.
  2. Near the water heater or the water meter.
  3. In a crawl space or an interior closet in homes without a basement.
  4. Outside, in a curb box, which is usually the utility's valve and not for homeowner use.
  5. Look for a lever handle or a round wheel on a pipe, often near a meter.

Valve types and how to close them

The type decides the motion.

Valve typeHow it looksTo close
Ball valveLever handleTurn the lever a quarter turn, so it sits across the pipe.
Gate valveRound wheel handleTurn clockwise until it stops. Do not force it.
Fixture shutoffSmall oval handle near a sink or toiletTurn clockwise to close that fixture only.
Meter valveValve next to the meterUse only if the main is stuck, and call the utility if unsure.

What to do after you shut it off

Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines, and flush a toilet once to empty the tank. This relieves pressure and stops water draining from the pipes into the leak. If the water heater is electric, turn off its breaker so the elements do not run dry.

Then photograph the damage and the source, and call dispatch. If the water came from a burst pipe, the page on burst pipes explains the restoration side.

When the main valve is stuck or leaking

Old valves can seize or leak around the handle. Do not force a gate valve with a wrench or hammer, since a broken valve is worse than a stuck one. If you cannot close the valve, contact the water utility and a plumber, and call dispatch for extraction while you wait.

A plumber can replace an old gate valve with a ball valve, which is easier to close in an emergency and less likely to seize.

Utility ownership in Waunakee

Waunakee Utilities is the village's locally owned electric and water utility. The part of the service line between the street and the meter is generally the utility's responsibility, and the line inside your property is yours. If you are unsure where the boundary sits, call the utility and ask before a leak happens.

Winters matter here too. Madison's January average is 19.4 degrees, and a split pipe from a freeze is the classic reason to find the main. See the frozen pipe page for what to do before the thaw. The same applies in Dane, DeForest and Mazomanie, although private wells in rural properties change the layout.

A five-minute drill for your household

  • Find the main shutoff and tag it with a bright label.
  • Close and open it once to confirm it moves.
  • Show everyone in the household where it is.
  • Locate the electrical panel and the water heater shutoffs.
  • Save the dispatch number in your phone.

Appliance and fixture shutoffs

The main valve is the big one, and the smaller valves matter too. Toilets, sinks, washing machines, dishwashers and the water heater each have their own shutoff. Closing one of them stops a local leak without cutting water to the whole house, which is faster and less disruptive when the problem is small.

Spend ten minutes walking through the house and finding each one. If a valve will not turn, a plumber can replace it, and you will know before the emergency.

Things to avoid in an emergency

  • Forcing a stuck valve with a wrench or a hammer.
  • Turning the water back on before the leak is repaired.
  • Running the water heater with the tank empty.
  • Standing in water while touching electrical.
  • Waiting for a plumber before starting extraction.

Label it and photograph it

Tie a bright tag to the main valve and take a photo of the valve and its surroundings. Share the photo with the household. In a stressful moment, people forget where the valve is, and a picture on the phone fixes that.

Water main questions

Where is the main water shutoff in most homes?

Usually where the water line enters the house, in the basement or utility room near the meter or water heater.

Should I open faucets after closing the main?

Yes, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure.

Who handles the water line between the street and my Waunakee meter?

That section is generally the utility's responsibility. Call Waunakee Utilities if you are unsure where the boundary is.

What if I cannot find the shutoff?

Call the water utility and a plumber, and call dispatch for extraction. Do not wait on the plumber to start the water removal.
