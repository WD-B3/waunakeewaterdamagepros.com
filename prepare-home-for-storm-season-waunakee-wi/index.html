How to Prepare Your Waunakee Home for Storm Season

A short checklist of the places water gets in, and what to do about each before the next heavy rain.

By the Waunakee Water Damage Pros teamUpdated November 2026

Most storm water damage starts with something small that was easy to fix beforehand: a clogged gutter, a dead sump battery, a loose flashing. Spending an afternoon on these checks costs far less than a flooded basement, and the best time to do them is before the forecast turns.

This guide goes through the weak points in the order water usually finds them. It is general guidance for Waunakee and the nearby towns, and a roofer, plumber or electrician should handle anything outside your comfort zone.

If a storm has already caused damage, skip to the storm damage cleanup page or call the 24/7 line.

The five checks that matter most

Each one targets a common way water enters.

Sump pump

Pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and discharges well away from the house. Test the float and consider a battery backup.

Gutters and downspouts

Clear debris and make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation.

Roof and flashing

Look for lifted shingles, cracked sealant and loose flashing around chimneys, vents and valleys.

Windows and window wells

Check seals and clear window wells so rain drains away.

Floor drains and sewer line

Clear floor drains and have the lateral scoped if backups have happened.

Why the sump pump deserves the most attention

A sump pump is the one piece of equipment that stands between a wet spring and a flooded basement, and it works hardest when the power is most likely to fail. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps it running during an outage. Testing it twice a year costs nothing.

The Wisconsin insurance commissioner notes that sump pump overflow generally needs a special endorsement, so a failed pump may not be covered without one. Check your policy and read the basement page to see what happens when it fails.

Indoor preparation

  • Keep valuables, documents and electronics off the basement floor, on shelves or in plastic bins.
  • Know where the main water valve is and how to turn it.
  • Know where the electrical panel is and keep it clear.
  • Photograph the home and your belongings now, so you have a record of their condition.
  • Keep your insurer's number and your policy number where you can find them.

Weather context for Waunakee

Dane County's August 2018 flooding followed record rainfall, and county reports counted more than $154 million in damage and 1,544 residences affected. The storm showed that a single heavy event can overwhelm drainage across the county, in neighborhoods that had never flooded before.

Madison's 1991 to 2020 normals, the nearest official benchmark for Waunakee, show about 37 inches of precipitation a year. Spring and summer storms deliver much of it in short bursts, and preparing for one heavy rain event is more useful than preparing for the average.

Flood coverage and floodplain checks

Dane County's 2017 hazard mitigation plan lists one structure in Waunakee in the 100-year floodplain and 22 in the 500-year floodplain. Check your own parcel on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. A National Flood Insurance Program policy generally has a 30-day waiting period, so decide before storm season, not during it.

The same applies in nearby towns. If you live in Mazomanie near Black Earth Creek, or in Dane or DeForest, the same checks apply.

What to do when a storm is forecast

Move valuables off the floor, clear storm drains near your home, charge the sump battery and keep your phone charged. During the storm, stay away from basements with standing water near electrical. Afterward, photograph any damage and call dispatch if water got in.

After the storm: a first-hour routine

  • Stay clear of downed power lines and call the utility or 911.
  • Check the basement from the stairs before stepping onto a wet floor.
  • Photograph the exterior and each wet room.
  • Shut off water if a pipe is damaged.
  • Call your insurer and then dispatch if water got in.

A kit worth keeping

Keep a flashlight with extra batteries, a battery-powered radio, work gloves, rubber boots, plastic sheeting, duct tape and a list of phone numbers in one place. Add a few contractor bags and a camera or fully charged phone. None of it is expensive, and all of it is easier to use when you do not have to look for it in the dark.

Talk to your neighbors

Storms hit blocks, not single houses. A neighbor who knows you have a sump pump, a key to your gutter ladder or a number to call can help in a hurry. Swap phone numbers, and tell each other when you are away. It costs nothing and often saves a basement.

Storm preparation questions

How often should I test my sump pump?

Twice a year is common, in early spring and early fall. Pour water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and discharges properly.

Do I need a battery backup for my sump?

Storms often cause power outages when the pump is needed most, so a backup lowers the risk. A plumber can advise on options.

Is my Waunakee address in a floodplain?

Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Dane County's 2017 plan counts one structure in the village in the 100-year floodplain and 22 in the 500-year floodplain.

What if a storm damages my roof?

Photograph it, arrange temporary protection and call dispatch for any water inside. A roofer repairs the roof itself.
