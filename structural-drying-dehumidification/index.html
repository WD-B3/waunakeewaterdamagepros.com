Water that looks gone is often still in the walls, subfloor and framing. If a surface feels cool or damp, the air smells musty, or paint is bubbling after a leak, the structure is probably still wet. A drying plan with equipment and measured readings is the only way to know when the building is truly dry.

Structural drying is the controlled removal of moisture from building materials using air movers, dehumidifiers and monitoring. It is not the same as extraction, which removes liquid water, and it is not a fan left running in a room. The goal is to bring materials to their dry standard, not just to make surfaces feel dry.

This service follows nearly every other page on this site. After a water extraction, a pipe failure or a basement loss, drying is what makes the repair safe to begin.

Symptoms of a structure that is still wet

You do not need a meter to notice trouble. A room that stays humid, windows that fog, baseboards that swell, floors that cup and a smell that returns after cleaning are all signs that moisture is still trapped. Water also climbs, so drywall can wick moisture a foot or more above the visible line.

If you notice any of these after a leak, call for a moisture assessment. Early drying is cheaper than late repair, and it reduces the chance that damp materials start to grow mold.

How a drying job is run

  1. Moisture meters, thermal imaging and a hygrometer establish which materials are wet and what the room air is doing.
  2. Air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers are placed to match the wet area, not just fill the room.
  3. The crew returns to read the same points each visit, adjusts equipment and removes anything that is not drying.
  4. Readings are logged against a dry reference so the end of the job is a number, not a guess.
  5. Equipment comes out only when the dry standard is met.

How long drying takes

Structural drying often runs three to five days, and larger or colder jobs run longer. Dense materials such as hardwood, plaster and concrete release moisture slowly, and cold weather slows evaporation. That is why the decision to stop comes from readings and not from a calendar.

Winters in the Waunakee area are cold, and Madison's January average is 19.4 degrees. A basement that stays cold may need a desiccant unit, and an exterior wall cavity can dry slowly. Crews plan equipment around that.

Structural drying questions

Can you check for hidden moisture the same day in Waunakee?

Same-day response is offered when crews are available. Call, describe the leak and dispatch schedules a moisture assessment.

Can I dry my house with fans and a store dehumidifier?

For a damp spot, sometimes. For a wall, floor or basement loss, consumer equipment rarely reaches the dry standard and moisture stays behind surfaces.

How do you know when the structure is dry?

By repeated readings compared with a dry reference material. The job ends when the numbers match, not when the surface feels dry.

Why do the machines run for days?

Moisture leaves materials slowly. Dense materials and cold weather extend the time, and the crew checks readings each visit.
