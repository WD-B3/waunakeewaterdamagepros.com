How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Waunakee Home

A meter test and a few checks can confirm a leak before it ruins a wall.

By the Waunakee Water Damage Pros teamUpdated November 2026

Most water damage starts with a leak nobody saw. A pinhole in a supply line or a loose connection can run for weeks behind a wall or under a floor, and the first sign is often a smell, a stain or a high bill. Finding the leak early keeps the damage small.

This guide walks through the checks a homeowner can do, starting with the water meter. It is general guidance, and a plumber locates and repairs the leak itself. Our crews handle the drying and damage afterward.

If you find water actively flowing, shut off the main and call.

The meter test

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water.
  2. Find your water meter and note the reading, or watch the leak indicator, which is a small triangle or dial.
  3. Wait 30 minutes without using water, then check again.
  4. If the reading moved or the indicator spins, water is leaking somewhere.
  5. Close the main valve and check again to see whether the leak is inside the house or between the meter and the house.

Where hidden leaks usually are

The most common hiding places are inside walls near bathrooms and kitchens, under floors below plumbing fixtures, in ceilings below bathrooms and in crawl spaces and basements where pipes run exposed. Outdoors, irrigation lines and hose bibs leak into foundations.

Appliance supply lines and the drain lines behind them are frequent culprits. See the appliance leak page, and the slab leak page for leaks under concrete.

What thermal imaging and meters add

A moisture meter reads moisture in building materials, and a thermal camera shows temperature differences that often mark wet areas. Together they let a crew map a leak without cutting the wall open first. They do not locate the leak in the pipe, which is a plumber's job.

Once the leak is found and repaired, the crew dries the structure and documents the readings. See structural drying for the process.

Waunakee context

Waunakee Utilities is the village's locally owned electric and water utility. If the meter test shows a leak between the meter and the house, ask the utility who is responsible for that section. Inside the house, the repair is yours.

Cold winters stress older lines, and Madison's January average is 19.4 degrees. Leaks often appear after a hard freeze. Homes in Dane, DeForest and Mazomanie on private wells have no city meter, so a leak shows up as a pump that runs too often, a drop in pressure or a wet spot, and a plumber can test the system.

When to call

Call a plumber when the meter test shows a leak, and call dispatch if there is wet material, stains or smell. The sooner the structure is dried, the less there is to repair. The Wisconsin insurance commissioner notes that gradual leaks are often not covered, so finding one early also protects your coverage position.

Quick checks around the house

  • Toilet tank: add a few drops of food coloring and see whether it appears in the bowl without flushing.
  • Faucets and shutoffs: look for drips, mineral crust or damp cabinet floors.
  • Water heater: check the base and the pressure relief discharge.
  • Outdoor spigots and irrigation: look for wet soil near the foundation.
  • HVAC: check the condensate line and drain pan.

After the leak is found

Fixing the pipe is only half the job. The wet materials still need drying, and the longer a leak ran, the more there is. A moisture map shows how far the water traveled, and a drying plan brings the structure back to normal.

Keep the plumber's report and your photos. If the leak damaged finished surfaces, they help support a claim if the cause is covered.

What not to do

Do not drill or cut into walls hunting for a leak without turning off water and power nearby. Do not assume a stain is directly under the source, since water travels. Do not ignore a high bill or a running meter because nothing looks wet.

Keeping a leak log

Write down the meter reading at the same time each morning for a week. A steady climb with no one home points to a leak, and the numbers help a plumber narrow the search. Note any new stain, smell or sound alongside the reading.

Hidden leak questions

How do I test for a hidden leak with my water meter?

Turn off all water use, note the reading, wait 30 minutes and check again. Movement means a leak.

What if I am on a private well in a rural area near Dane?

There is no utility meter, so look for a pump that runs often, a drop in pressure or a wet spot, and have a plumber test the system.

Can you find the leak itself?

A plumber locates and repairs the leak. We map moisture and dry the damage.

Does insurance cover a hidden leak?

Sudden leaks are often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Ask your insurer.
