What the 2017 hazard plan says about drainage in DeForest

The Dane County hazard mitigation plan lists two DeForest structures in the 100-year floodplain and four in the 500-year floodplain, with none in the floodway. It also describes the Yahara River corridor running through the village and a village stormwater utility that manages drainage.

The plan names two local drainage trouble spots. East of USH 51, runoff from several hundred acres of farmland squeezes through a 48-inch culvert, and the plan lists four Renata Court homes as threatened, though it says none have flooded in past storms. Near Dahl Park, stormwater can back up from a 36-inch storm sewer.

What this means for a DeForest homeowner

The mapped flood exposure is small, but the drainage notes show that stormwater can be a local issue even away from the river. A wet basement in DeForest can come from a sump failure, a sewer backup, a roof leak or runoff, and each has its own page. Check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Most homeowner policies do not cover flooding or seepage through the foundation, according to the Wisconsin insurance commissioner, so ask your insurer about a separate flood policy before a loss.

If water is still spreading, before the crew is on site

Five steps that limit the damage.

Close the valve

The main valve first, then fixture valves. If the leak is a sump overflow, keep clear of the pit.

Power down carefully

Breakers to wet areas only if the panel is on dry floor. Otherwise call the utility or 911.

Protect belongings

Lift inventory, equipment and files off the floor. In a shop, move stock away from floor drains.

Avoid contaminated water

Treat drain, storm and sump water as contaminated. Keep children and pets away.
