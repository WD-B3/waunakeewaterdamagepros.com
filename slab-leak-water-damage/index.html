A slab leak is a leak in a water line buried under or inside a concrete slab, and it is an emergency when water is showing at the surface. If the meter is running with every fixture off, or a floor feels warm or damp, close the main water valve and call the 24/7 line. Do not break concrete yourself.

Slab leak water damage is the wetting of flooring and walls by water rising from beneath. It is less common in Wisconsin than in slab-on-grade regions, because many Waunakee homes have basements and the slab is the basement floor, but houses with slab areas, additions and garages can still have lines under concrete.

The leak itself is found and repaired by a plumber with leak-location tools. Our role is the drying and building repair, with a link to the broader extraction and structural drying work.

Slab leak emergency: the signs to act on first

Quiet leaks run for months. These signs mean one is already active.

  • The water meter keeps moving after every fixture is off.
  • A floor feels warm in one spot, which can point to a hot water line leak.
  • The sound of running water when everything is closed.
  • Damp flooring, lifted laminate or wet baseboards without a visible source.

How slab leaks damage a house

Water from a slab line rises through the concrete and cracks and soaks the flooring above. It can lift laminate, stain carpet and cup wood, and it may travel along the slab edge into wall bottoms. Because the source is hidden, damage often grows before the cause is understood.

A cause outside the house matters too. A leak in the service line between the meter and the house may be the water utility's or the owner's responsibility depending on where it sits, and the village or Waunakee Utilities can say who owns which section.

How we work around a slab leak

  • A plumber locates and repairs the leak, using acoustic or thermal tools, so the water is confirmed off first.
  • We extract standing water and map moisture in the flooring, subfloor and wall bottoms.
  • Wet flooring that cannot dry is removed and the slab and framing are dried.
  • Readings are logged, and the cause and repair invoice help your insurance claim.

What affects slab leak damage costs

The repair is a plumber's cost, and the water damage follows its own scope.

What the plumber does and what happens next

A plumber uses acoustic and thermal tools to locate a leak under concrete, then repairs the line, reroutes it overhead or replaces it, depending on the pipe condition and the layout. Breaking the slab is usually the last resort. Some homes have in-floor heating lines, which can also leak and need their own diagnosis.

Once the line is sound, drying can begin in earnest. Flooring above the slab is removed if it cannot dry, the slab surface is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers, and readings are logged until they hold steady.

What to share with your insurer

  • The plumber's leak report and invoice.
  • Photos of the damage and the repair.
  • Your water bills before and after, if they changed.
  • Moisture readings and the scope of drying.
  • The date you first noticed signs, which helps establish whether the event was sudden.

Related damage to check

Water from a slab leak spreads under flooring, so hardwood floor repair and carpet drying are common next steps. A burst pipe elsewhere in the house is handled in a similar way.
