A water loss in a business is an emergency for more than the building. Every hour of downtime costs revenue, and wet inventory, equipment and records lose value quickly. Shut off the water, move inventory and electronics off the floor if it is safe, photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line before closing for the night.

Commercial water damage restoration is the extraction, drying and repair of commercial and multi-unit properties after water loss. It uses the same methods as residential work but with larger equipment, tighter schedules and heavier documentation, because tenants, landlords and insurers all need a clear record.

If the cause is a pipe, an appliance or a storm, the relevant pages explain the source. See burst pipes, storm damage and sewage backups for what each involves.

Commercial water loss: first steps

Protect people, then property, then the schedule.

  • Shut off the water at the main or the affected branch, and confirm it with the building contact.
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet areas and away from electrical. Call the utility or 911 if water is near the panel.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics up and out of the water if it is safe.
  • Photograph the damage and note times, then call dispatch.

How commercial jobs are run

  • Dispatch arranges a walkthrough with the owner or property manager and agrees the scope and priorities.
  • Crews extract and dry in sequence, often working around operating hours to limit downtime.
  • Moisture readings and photos are logged, which supports claims by owners and tenants.
  • Communication with the property manager continues through the job, and repair scope is estimated when drying finishes.

Landlords, tenants and insurance

In a multi-unit building, the source unit, the unit below and the common areas may fall under different policies. Documentation that shows where water started and where it traveled helps each party and each insurer sort it out. We record moisture maps and photographs, and the coverage decision stays with the insurer.

Business interruption coverage, if you carry it, often depends on the cause and the policy wording. Ask your agent before work begins so the right records are kept from the first day.

What property managers should ask for

A clear record protects the owner, the tenant and the insurer.

  • A written scope listing the areas affected and the work in each.
  • Moisture maps and daily or visit-by-visit readings.
  • Photographs that show the loss before, during and after.
  • A schedule that names the work hours and any areas that must stay closed.
  • A list of materials removed, with quantities where possible.

Keeping a business running during drying

Not every loss closes a building. Crews can often isolate the wet area with containment, run equipment overnight and keep the front of the shop open. Noise, airflow and cords across walkways are the practical trade-offs, and they are easier to manage when the manager names the busiest hours at the start.

For restaurants and food businesses, local health rules decide when a space may reopen. Ask the health authority before reopening, and keep the restoration records to show what was done.
