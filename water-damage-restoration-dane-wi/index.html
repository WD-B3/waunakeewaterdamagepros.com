When water is spreading in Dane

Emergency water removal is available in Dane around the clock. While you wait for a crew, work through these steps.

  • Close the main water valve, or the valve at the leaking fixture.
  • If water has reached outlets, the panel or the furnace, stay out and call your electric utility or 911 before you call us.
  • Lift valuables, papers and small furniture off wet floors, and photograph the damage first.
  • Keep people and pets out of water that may be sewage, storm runoff or sump overflow.

If water is still spreading, do these first

Each card is a step you can take in the minutes before a crew can be on site.

Stop the source

Shut off the main valve or the fixture valve. For a sump failure, unplug the pump only if the floor around the outlet is dry.

Cut power safely

Turn off breakers to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry floor. Otherwise leave it and call the utility.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes, electronics and papers up and out of the water. Blocks or foil under furniture legs help.

Keep clear

Children and pets stay out of any water that may be contaminated, including basement water after heavy rain.

The water problems Dane homes face

A village this old has houses from many eras, and older houses tend to have older plumbing, stone or block foundations and basements that were never built to stay dry. The usual causes are a supply line that fails, a sump pump that quits during the spring melt and a roof that leaks in a hard storm. Each of those has its own page on this site.

For a wet lower level, read about basement water damage cleanup. For a split pipe, see burst pipe water damage and frozen pipe damage. For a drain that backs up, see sewage backup cleanup.

Properties outside the village add a few differences. A private well and septic system change what a backup or a flood means for the house, and barns, garages and outbuildings can take water too. Tell the call-taker what is on the property.

Flood and storm context for Dane

Dane County's August 2018 flooding followed record rainfall, and county reports counted more than $154 million in damage and 1,544 residences affected. It showed that heavy rain can flood basements across the county, including in small villages far from a mapped floodplain.

Madison's 1991 to 2020 normals, the nearest official benchmark, show about 37 inches of precipitation and 52 inches of snow a year, with a January average of 19.4 degrees. Search your parcel on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your insurer about sump overflow and sewer backup endorsements, since the Wisconsin insurance commissioner says those are not part of a standard homeowners policy.

How a crew works a Dane job

  • Dispatch confirms the address, the source and any hazards.
  • A crew extracts standing water and maps moisture with meters and thermal imaging.
  • Wet materials that cannot dry are removed, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest to a measured standard.
  • Photos, readings and a written scope support your insurance claim. Coverage decisions stay with your insurer.
